About the Data

This data was originally compiled from the Game of Thrones television show for this research article:

Lystad RP, Brown BT. “Death is certain, the time is not”: mortality and survival in Game of Thrones. Injury Epidemiology 2018;5:44.

The data comes from seasons 1-8 and originally has two data sets within it: a character data set and an episode data set. However for this analysis, only the character data set was used. The character data set contains 359 observations (characters) and 35 variables, including information about sociodemographics, exposures, and mortality.

Now, lets see if there’s any of these characteristics significantly influence a character’s chance at survival. Table 3 contains information from a multivariate logistic regression determining the effect of certain characteristics on a characters mortality while holding the others constant. Because several categories within religion or allegiance have small sample sizes or complete mortality/survival (e.g., zero deaths in a specific subgroup), standard logistic regression suffers from separation. Fitting the model using Firth’s penalized likelihood approach reduces small-sample bias and produces finite, reliable parameter estimates.

There are many variables that are significantly associated with mortality. For instance, being in the Night’s Watch at the time of death was significantly associated with death (4.08 (95% CI 1.48, 12.3; p=0.006)). Meaning members of the Night’s Watch have 4.08 times the odds of dying than other allegiances. And this makes sense considering the Battle of Winterfell.
